## Dataset

This analysis used data from the General Social Survey (GSS) 2018 dataset. The project examined the relationship between a mother's highest year of school completed and the number of children.

Source:
https://gss.norc.org/

## Key Findings

- A Pearson correlation analysis was conducted to examine the relationship between maternal education level and number of children.
- The analysis revealed a statistically significant negative correlation between the two variables (r = -0.229, p < .001).
- Results indicated that mothers with higher levels of education tended to have fewer children.
- Although statistically significant, the relationship was relatively weak in strength.
- Scatterplot analysis showed a negative linear trend between education level and family size.
- The large sample size (N = 2088) increased the reliability of the findings.
- Results suggested a meaningful association between maternal education and number of children within this sample.
