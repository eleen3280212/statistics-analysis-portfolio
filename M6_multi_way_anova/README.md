## Dataset

This analysis used data from the General Social Survey (GSS) 2018 dataset. The project examined whether educational attainment differed across racial groups and gender groups, and whether an interaction effect existed between race and sex.

Source:
https://gss.norc.org/

## Key Findings

- A two-way ANOVA was conducted to examine the effects of race and sex on highest year of school completed.
- Levene’s Test was significant (p < .001), indicating that the homogeneity of variance assumption was violated and results should be interpreted with caution.
- Race had a statistically significant main effect on educational attainment, F(2, 2339) = 25.461, p < .001.
- Sex did not have a statistically significant main effect on educational attainment, F(1, 2339) = 0.104, p = .747.
- The interaction effect between race and sex was not statistically significant, F(2, 2339) = 0.380, p = .684.
- Bonferroni post hoc comparisons showed significant differences among all racial groups.
- White respondents reported the highest average years of schooling, followed by Black respondents, while respondents in the Other category reported the lowest educational attainment.
- The interaction plot indicated that the relationship between race and educational attainment was generally consistent across males and females.
