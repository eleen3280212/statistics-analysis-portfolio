## Dataset

This analysis used data from the General Social Survey (GSS) 2018 dataset. The project examined whether educational attainment differed significantly across racial groups.

Source:
https://gss.norc.org/

## Key Findings

- A one-way ANOVA was conducted to compare the mean highest year of school completed among White, Black, and Other racial groups.
- Levene’s Test of Homogeneity of Variances was significant (p < .001), indicating that the equal variance assumption was violated.
- Welch’s ANOVA was therefore used and revealed statistically significant differences in educational attainment among racial groups (Welch's F = 18.64, p < .001).
- White respondents reported the highest average years of schooling, followed by Black respondents, while respondents in the Other category reported the lowest average educational attainment.
- Bonferroni post hoc comparisons showed that all pairwise group differences were statistically significant.
- Error bar chart analysis visually supported the statistical results, showing clear separation among the group means.
- Results suggested that race was associated with statistically significant differences in educational attainment within this sample.
